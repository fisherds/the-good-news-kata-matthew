# 1.1 You can make me clean (8:1–4)

Our first translated unit. Unit file: `translation/matthew/kata-matthew-8-1-4.usx`.

---

## 8:1 — Coming down the mountain

> Καταβάντος δὲ αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄρους ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ ὄχλοι πολλοί.

| Greek | Sounds like | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Καταβάντος | ka-ta-BAN-tos | having come down | *kata* "down" + *bainō* "go". Tim: "came down" (7:25) |
| δὲ | deh | now | a light joining word; never first in its clause |
| αὐτοῦ | ow-TOO | he / him | *auto-* as in "automatic" (self) |
| ἀπὸ τοῦ ὄρους | a-PO too O-roos | from the mountain | the mountain of 5:1 |
| ἠκολούθησαν | ay-ko-LOO-thay-san | they followed | *akoloutheō*, Matthew's discipleship word |
| ὄχλοι πολλοί | OKH-loy pol-LOY | many crowds | *polloi* as in "polygon" |

**Grammar:** Greek can hang "him having come down" on the sentence as a phrase on its own. English says "when he came down".

**Literary find:** 8:1 ties back to two verses.

- 5:1 — he saw the crowds and went **up onto the mountain**.
- 4:25 — **many crowds followed him**, the same ὄχλοι πολλοί with the same verb.

The Sermon is framed by going up and coming down. "Followed" comes back in all four unnumbered rows (8:19, 22, 23; 9:9 "Follow me").

## 8:2 — "If you want, you can"

> καὶ ἰδοὺ λεπρὸς προσελθὼν προσεκύνει αὐτῷ λέγων· Κύριε, ἐὰν θέλῃς δύνασαί με καθαρίσαι.

| Greek | Sounds like | Meaning | Tim's word |
|---|---|---|---|
| καὶ ἰδοὺ | kai i-DOO | and look | "look" (never "behold") |
| λεπρὸς | lep-ROS | a skin-diseased man | from *lepis*, "scale" |
| προσελθὼν | pros-el-THOHN | approaching | "approaching" (4:3) |
| προσεκύνει | pros-e-KOO-nay | he was bowing down | "bow down", the Magi's word (2:2, 2:11) |
| λέγων | LEH-gohn | saying | |
| Κύριε | KOO-ree-eh | Master | "Master, master!" (7:21) |
| ἐὰν θέλῃς | eh-AN THEL-ace | if you want | *thelō* "want" (5:40) |
| δύνασαί | DOO-na-sai | you are able | root of "dynamite" |
| με καθαρίσαι | meh ka-tha-REE-sai | to make me clean | "catharsis" |

- **7:21 has just said** that not everyone who says "Master, master!" will enter the kingdom. The very next person to say "Master" is an outcast, and he is welcomed.
- **The last people to bow down** to Jesus were the Magi, outsiders too.

## 8:3 — "I want: be clean"

> καὶ ἐκτείνας τὴν χεῖρα ἥψατο αὐτοῦ λέγων· Θέλω, καθαρίσθητι· καὶ εὐθέως ἐκαθαρίσθη αὐτοῦ ἡ λέπρα.

| Greek | Sounds like | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ἐκτείνας τὴν χεῖρα | ek-TAY-nas tayn KHAY-ra | stretching out **the** hand | Greek says "the hand" where English says "his hand" |
| ἥψατο αὐτοῦ | HAYP-sa-to ow-TOO | he touched him | touching him should make Jesus unclean (Leviticus 13:45–46); here cleanness goes the other way |
| Θέλω | THEL-oh | I want | his answer repeats the man's word |
| καθαρίσθητι | ka-tha-RIS-thay-tee | be clean! | a command in the passive |
| εὐθέως | yoo-THEH-ohs | immediately | Tim 4:20, 22 |
| ἐκαθαρίσθη | e-ka-tha-RIS-thay | was made clean | |
| αὐτοῦ ἡ λέπρα | ow-TOO hay LEP-ra | his skin disease | |

**Greek endings:** the three "clean"s are one verb in three forms. The ending shows who is doing what and when, not a change of meaning.

| Form | Grammar | Literally |
|---|---|---|
| καθαρίσαι | infinitive, active | to make clean |
| καθαρίσθητι | command, passive | be (made) clean! |
| ἐκαθαρίσθη | past, passive | was made clean |

## 8:4 — "Show yourself to the priest"

> καὶ λέγει αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Ὅρα μηδενὶ εἴπῃς, ἀλλὰ ὕπαγε σεαυτὸν δεῖξον τῷ ἱερεῖ, καὶ προσένεγκον τὸ δῶρον ὃ προσέταξεν Μωϋσῆς εἰς μαρτύριον αὐτοῖς.

| ὁ Ἰησοῦς | ho ee-ay-SOOS | Iesous | |

**Echo:** in 5:23–24 Jesus talked about presenting an offering (be reconciled first, then present it). Here a man who was shut out can present it again.

## What we decided

- **"Now"** for δέ (8:1), so it stays apart from καί "and". Every one of the other five "and"s is a καί in the Greek.
- **"many crowds"** and **"followed him"**, matching Tim 4:25. "Follow after" is saved for ἀκολουθέω ὀπίσω (10:38). Not "walked" (that's περιπατέω, 9:5) or "apprenticed" (that's for μαθητής, disciple).
- **"skin-diseased *man*" / "skin disease"**: the Levitical sense, not only Hansen's disease. The parts of speech match the Greek: λεπρὸς (8:2) is an **adjective** standing alone as a person ("a skin-diseased one", like "the poor"), and λέπρα (8:3) is the **noun**. Greek needs no word for "man"; the masculine ending -ος says it. English needs one, so *man* is supplied, in light italics. "A skin-diseased one" was the option if we wanted nothing supplied.
- **"approaching, he bowed down to him"**: bowing down is the main verb; approaching is the participle. "He" is already inside the Greek verb, the same as "he touched him".
- **"clean" three times**, each with "clean / pure" in its Greek bracket: "Be clean (καθαρίσθητι clean / pure)". No form means "pure" more than another, so all three get it.
- **Supplied words in light italics** (like the NASB): *man* (8:2; λεπρὸς is "a skin-diseased one", male from the ending) and *that* (8:4; Ὅρα μηδενὶ εἴπῃς is literally "see, tell no one"). Not "he" (inside the verb) or "a" (Greek has no word for it).
- **"Iesous", "Mouses"**: Greek name forms, like Tim's.
- **"the priest"** (singular), **"witness"**: the whole μαρτυρ- family gets "witness", like Tim's "birth" for γεννάω and γένεσις.
- **Boxes:** 8:1 bridge (light box) / the healing in one strong box with thin lines: the man | the ask | the answer | the result / 8:4 the sending (light box). No sidebar letters.
- **The ask and the answer share the same indents**, so the parallel shows:

```
approaching,                          And stretching out the hand,
he bowed down to him, saying,         he touched him, saying,
   “Master, if you want,                 “I want.
   you are able to make me clean.”       Be clean.”
```

## Tidbits

### "Tell no one", yet "a witness"

Matthew has five commands to keep quiet:

| | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 8:4 | the cleansed man | "…but go, show yourself, as a witness" |
| 9:30 | two blind men | they disobey and spread it everywhere (9:31) |
| 12:16 | the crowds he healed | Matthew explains it with Isaiah 42: the servant "will not quarrel or cry out" |
| 16:20 | the disciples | don't tell anyone he is the Messiah |
| 17:9 | Peter, James, John | "…**until** the Son of Humanity is raised" |

People expected a war-leading Messiah, so news of miracles would draw the wrong crowd for the wrong reasons. Jesus wants to be known by what he does: the man's healed body *is* the witness. And 17:9 shows the silence has an end date. The last command in the book is "**Go** and make disciples of all nations" (28:19). Scholars call this the "messianic secret"; it's even stronger in Mark.

### "Them" is plural

The plural rules out the one priest. Most likely it means the people: the crowds of 8:1 and the town that shut him out, which can take him back once the priest declares him clean. It could also mean the priests as a group, as proof that Jesus keeps the Torah (5:17). Matthew uses the same phrase again in 10:18, "as a witness to them and to the nations".

### Stretching out *the* hand (your insight)

Greek says "the hand", not "his hand", and keeping it opens up a picture of a king.

- **Esther 4:11, 5:2:** anyone who approaches the king uninvited dies, unless the king holds out the golden scepter in his hand. Here the outcast approaches and bows, and the king stretches out the hand.
- **Exodus 3:20:** in the Greek Old Testament, God says "**stretching out the hand** (ἐκτείνας τὴν χεῖρα), I will strike the Egyptians". These are Matthew's exact words (checked against the Greek Old Testament). The verse before says Pharaoh won't let them go "except by a mighty hand" (3:19). There the hand strikes Pharaoh; here it heals. It became our first cross-reference (the ⓘ after "the hand"; see "Systems" below). See also 7:5 and the "mighty hand and outstretched arm" of Deuteronomy 4:34 and 5:15.

**The same four words elsewhere**, found with `python3 tools/lxx.py -s "εκτεινας την χειρα"`:

| Where | Whose hand | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 8:9 | Noah | takes in the dove that found no rest and brings her into the ark |
| Exodus 3:20 | God | strikes Egypt "with all my wonders" (θαυμάσια) |
| Matthew 8:3 | Jesus | touches the outcast: clean |
| Matthew 12:49 | Jesus | toward his disciples: "Look, my mother and my brothers" |
| Matthew 14:31 | Jesus | catches Peter, who is sinking |
| Matthew 26:51 | a disciple | draws a sword and **strikes** (πατάξας, Exodus 3:20's verb) the high priest's servant, and Jesus stops him |

The Exodus hand strikes. Every time Jesus stretches out the hand, it rescues or welcomes. In Gethsemane a disciple reaches for the Exodus way, and Jesus refuses it. Matthew's only other use of θαυμάσια, "wonders", is in 21:15: the chief priests see "the wonders he did" in the Temple, healing the blind and the lame, and they are angry.

This is why we bold **the hand**.

### "I want", not "I am willing"

"I am willing" is smoother English, but "I want" says that Jesus *wants* this man clean. It also keeps the echo of the man's "if you want".

### Three outsiders in a row

The leper (8:2), the Gentile centurion (8:5) and a woman (8:14). None of them could go into the inner courts of the Temple, and Jesus goes to all three first.
