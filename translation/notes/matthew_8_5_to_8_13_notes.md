## 8:7 — Statement, or question?

> καὶ λέγει αὐτῷ· Ἐγὼ ἐλθὼν θεραπεύσω αὐτόν.

| Greek | Sounds like | Form | Meaning | Note | Alternative phrases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| καὶ λέγει αὐτῷ | kai LEH-gay ow-TOH | “and” + present verb, he (historical present) + “to him”, dative | and he said to him | Tim renders this present "said" (4:6, 10, 19) | Jesus said to him <sup>NASB NET NIV</sup> · And he said to him <sup>ESV</sup> |
| Ἐγὼ | eh-GOH | pronoun “I”, added for emphasis | **I** | Greek verbs already contain "I", so writing ἐγώ is emphatic | none of the four marks the emphasis in English |
| ἐλθὼν θεραπεύσω αὐτόν | el-THOHN the-ra-PYOO-soh ow-TON | aorist participle + future verb, I (-σω) + “him”, object | coming, I will heal him | *therapeuō*, as in "therapy". Tim "heal" (4:23–24) | I will come and heal him <sup>NASB ESV NET</sup> · Shall I come and heal him? <sup>NIV</sup> |

**Greek lesson:** the oldest manuscripts have no question marks; editors add them. This line can be read two ways:

- **"I will come and heal him."** Jesus agrees at once.
- **"Shall *I* come and heal him?"** A Jewish teacher entering a Gentile's house would be unclean. The emphatic "I" sounds like surprise, and Jesus does the same with another Gentile in 15:21–28.

The centurion's answer fits either: "I'm not worthy for you to come under my roof." **Choice 4.**

## Patterns worth highlighting

**Repeated words** (Greek shown at each one):

- **under** ×3: roof, authority, me. The core of his argument.
- **boy** ×3: 8:6, 8:8, 8:13.
- **will be cured / was cured**: promise, then fulfillment.
- **trust / trusted**: a page-wide color.
- **Go!/goes · Come!/comes · Do!/does**: three pairs, each its own match.
- **thrown down / thrown out**: same root.
- *Maybe:* **say a word / I say to this one / Truly I say to you**, whose word has authority. Possibly too many highlights.

**Page-wide colors:** those following (purple) · Go (purple, like 8:4) · amazed · trust.

**One-off echoes:** authority (7:29) · not worthy (John, 3:11) · east (the Magi) · darkness (4:16) · let it be done (6:10).

## A structure to react to

Boxes and thin lines, no letters:

```
┌ 8:5–6   The plea: approaching, "Master, my boy has been thrown down…"
│ ─────
└ 8:7     Jesus: "I will come and heal him" (or "Shall I…?")
┏ 8:8–9   The centurion's word:
┃           not worthy / under my roof / just say a word
┃         ─────
┃           for I too am under authority, with soldiers under me:
┃              "Go!"  and he goes
┃              "Come!" and he comes
┗              "Do this!" and he does
┌ 8:10–12 Jesus' word: amazed / "such trust"
│         ─────
│           many from east and west → recline with Abra'am…
└           the sons of the kingdom → thrown out into the darkness
┏ 8:13    "Go. As you have trusted, let it be done for you."
┗           And the boy was cured in that hour.
```

The middle is two speeches facing each other: the centurion's word about authority, and Jesus' word about trust. 8:11 and 8:12 sit at the same indent, so "in with Abra'am" and "out into the darkness" read as a pair.

## Choices

1. **ἑκατόνταρχος:** "centurion" (familiar) or "hundred-commander" (Tim-style compound)?
2. **παρακαλέω:** "pleading with him"? It breaks Tim's "comfort" (5:4), but that's a different sense. The root is "calling alongside".
3. **παῖς:** "boy" (keeps the ambiguity between son and servant; Matthew uses "slave" separately in 8:9), "servant", or "son"? The row title says "son".
4. **8:7:** statement ("I will come and heal him") or question ("Shall *I* come and heal him?")?
5. **Two healing words:** θεραπεύω "heal" (Tim); ἰάομαι "cure"? *Decided: "healed" for both (see 8:8–9).* "Made well" is saved for σῴζω (9:21–22).
6. **Two "go" verbs:** the centurion's πορεύομαι and Jesus' ὑπάγω. Tim uses "go" for both; keep "go" for both and show the Greek on each.
7. **βάλλω / ἐκβάλλω:** "thrown down" (8:6) and "thrown out" (8:12)? It carries forward: demons "thrown out" (8:16, 31; 9:33), and the Lord of the harvest "throws out workers" (9:38). Tim varies ("take out" the speck, "cast out" demons), so it's our call.

## Tenses: "approaching" and "approached"

Both are the **same tense** (aorist, a single completed action). What differs is the kind of verb form:

| Greek | Form | Job in the sentence | Ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| προσελθὼν (8:2) | aorist **participle** | describes the man on the way to the main action | approaching |
| προσῆλθεν (8:5) | aorist **finite verb** | the main action itself | approached |

English "-ing" here shows a participle, not the present tense; it's how Tim renders προσελθὼν (4:3) and how we did ἐκτείνας, "stretching out" (8:3, also aorist). We considered "having approached" and kept "-ing": the aorist sees an action as one whole event; it doesn't say "before". "Having" adds a time claim the Greek doesn't always make: ἀποκριθεὶς … ἔφη (8:8) is "responding, he said", not "having responded, he said", and the same Greek form needs the same English everywhere. When a participle opens a sentence with its own subject (the genitive at the start of 8:1 and 8:5), English uses "when…": "Now when he entered". Rule: **aorist participle → "-ing"; aorist finite verb → simple past; opening genitive participle → "when…"**.

## What we decided (8:5–6)

- **"Now when he entered into Kapharnaoum"**: δέ "Now", like 8:1. Εἰσελθόντος is aorist, so "entered", not "was entering": the same shape as "Now when he came down from the mountain".
- **"a centurion / approached him, / pleading with him and saying"**: here the main verb is προσῆλθεν "approached", and "pleading" and "saying" are participles, the reverse of 8:2 ("approaching, he bowed down"). The lines still parallel 8:2. "With" isn't italicized: English needs it to say "plead with" (same rule as "when"). "And saying" keeps the καί; verse 6 starts on it.
- **"my boy (παῖς son / servant)"**: like clean / pure, one word in the text and the second meanings in the bracket. Blue, three times (8:6, 8, 13).
- **"has been thrown down"**: βέβληται is a perfect, a past action whose result still stands. "Is thrown down" could sound like it's happening now. Light red, paired with ἐκβληθήσονται "thrown out" (8:12).
- **"in the house"**: the Greek has "the" (τῇ), not "my".
- **Plead is page-wide**: light violet on "pleading with" (8:5), and on παρεκάλουν (8:31, the demons) and παρεκάλεσαν (8:34, the town begs him to leave). Three pleas: for healing, for escape, for his absence.
- **Approach is page-wide**: light turquoise on "approaching" (8:2) and "approached" (8:5), and in the Greek of 8:19, 25; 9:14, 20, 28 (marked now).
- **Boxes, first pass, parallel to 8:1–4:** 8:5a bridge (light box) / the plea, Jesus' answer, and the centurion's word in one strong box with thin lines / Jesus to his followers, 8:10–12 (light box, an aside) / 8:13 the "Go" and the cure (strong box). The two strong boxes are the healing; the light box between them is the teaching.

## What we decided (8:7)

- **A question: "I, coming, will heal him?"** No manuscript has punctuation, so the Greek allows both. We chose the question for two reasons. (1) The emphatic Ἐγώ: "*I* am to come into a Gentile's house?" (Acts 10:28). (2) Its pair, the Canaanite woman (15:21–28), the other healing from a distance. There Jesus puts up a barrier first, and her humble answer draws out "great is your faith", then the same γενηθήτω σοι "let it be to you" and ἰάθη … "that hour" as 8:13. It's a gentle push that invites the centurion's answer. NIV has the question; NASB, ESV and NET have the statement.
- **Word order kept: "I, coming, will heal him?"** "I" first shows Ἐγώ's weight; "coming" follows the aorist-participle rule. The words don't change between the statement and the question, only the mark at the end, as in Greek.
- **"coming", not "going"**: ἔρχομαι can mean either (Tim: "we have come" 2:2, but "until going" 2:9 for this very form, ἐλθὼν). We keep "come" here because in 8:9 the centurion sets the two verbs against each other: Πορεύθητι "Go" / Ἔρχου "Come". "Go" is also our purple πορεύομαι / ὑπάγω. The unstated "into a Gentile's house" is said aloud by the centurion in 8:8: εἰσέλθῃς "come in under my roof", the same verb with εἰς- "into".
- **"And he said to him"**: λέγει is a present ("says", the *historical present*, a storyteller's way to bring a scene close). Tim renders it "said" (4:6, 4:10, 4:19), and so do we. SBLGNT has no "Iesous" here (TR adds ὁ Ἰησοῦς).

## What we decided (8:8–9)

- **"And responding, the centurion said, / “Master, I am not worthy that you come in under my roof,"**: every word of the Greek, with the "Master" of 8:2, 8:6. "That you come" is the English subjunctive, so no italics (like 8:4 "See *that* you tell no one", where "that" *is* supplied because Greek has no ἵνα). "Come in" keeps εἰσέλθῃς, his answer to "I, coming…?". A cross-reference ⓘ links "I am not worthy" to John the Baptist (3:11).
- **"but only speak with a word,"**: εἰπὲ is a command; Tim's "speak" (4:3). λόγῳ "with a word" (8:16).
- **"and my boy will be healed."**: we first drafted "cured" to keep ἰάομαι apart from θεραπεύω "heal" (8:7). The user chose "healed": "cured" sounds thin. The two verbs now share an English word; the light green and the Greek bracket on ἰάομαι (8:8, 8:13) keep them apart. Worth knowing: in Matthew's stories ἰάομαι is used only for the two Gentiles healed from a distance (8:8, 13; 15:28), plus the Isaiah quote in 13:15.
- **"For I too am a man under authority,"**: γάρ "for", καί "too". Not "and because".
- **Highlights:** under ×3 (light orange) · authority (orange text, **page-wide**: 8:9, 9:6, 9:8) · Go / goes (purple, page-wide go, now also 8:32 Ὑπάγετε and 9:13 πορευθέντες) · Come / comes (light cyan, the ἔρχομαι thread: 8:5 entered, 8:7 coming, 8:8 come in) · Do this / does (light tan).
- **Grammar:** the commands are imperatives (Πορεύθητι, Ποίησον aorist; Ἔρχου present); goes / comes / does are presents of habit, "every time".
- **Boxes:** one strong box around 8:5b–9 with thin lines between the plea, the question, the request (8:8) and the reason (8:9).

## Revised after review (8:5–9)

- **Greek brackets trimmed.** Kept only where a match is in sight: approached (προσῆλθεν, with 8:2's approaching), boy (παῖς son / servant, the gloss trick), coming / Come / comes (ἐλθὼν, Ἔρχου, ἔρχεται). Then put back on the three unders (ὑπὸ, ὑπὸ, ὑπʼ): three matches in sight, so the rule keeps them. Removed from pleading with, thrown down, come in, authority, Go / goes, Do this / does, healed. Highlights stay.
- **"entered into Kapharnaoum"**: tan text, no Greek, matching 8:1 "came down from the mountain". So the cyan "come" thread starts at 8:7.
- **Far links became ⓘ cross-references:** "has been thrown down" → 9:2 and 9:6 (story 6: paralyzed, thrown down, authority, go); "will be healed" → 15:28 (ἰάομαι, the other Gentile healed from a distance). Both use the BSB for the other passage until we translate it.
- **"speak with a word", not "speak a word"**: λόγῳ is dative, the *means* ("by / with a word"), not the object of "speak". The same form in 8:16: he throws out the spirits λόγῳ, "with a word". Kept.
- **Words with an ⓘ are bold**: "I am not worthy", "has been thrown down", "will be healed" (like 8:3's "stretching out the hand"). "Will be healed" gets its Greek back (ἰαθήσεται): the ⓘ is all about that word, and it matches 8:13's ἰάθη.
- **Thin lines restored** before "approached him" (8:5) and "approaching" (8:2): the user wants the line in 8:1–4, and 8:5–13 keeps it to stay parallel.
- **Master is page-wide dark grey** (`black` highlight, Tim's color for κύριος in 4:7, 10 and 7:21–22), no Greek: the English is the same word each time. 8:2, 6, 8, and in the Greek 8:21, 25; 9:28, 38.
- **8:9 moved one level left** (the reason, under the request of 8:8; the three commands one level in).
- **Boxes and thin lines:** still a first pass; the user isn't sold yet.
