## 8:7 — Statement, or question?

> καὶ λέγει αὐτῷ· Ἐγὼ ἐλθὼν θεραπεύσω αὐτόν.

| Greek | Sounds like | Meaning | Note | Alternative phrases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ἐγὼ | eh-GOH | **I** | Greek verbs already contain "I", so writing ἐγώ is emphatic | none of the four marks the emphasis in English |
| ἐλθὼν θεραπεύσω αὐτόν | el-THOHN the-ra-PYOO-soh ow-TON | coming, I will heal him | *therapeuō*, as in "therapy". Tim "heal" (4:23–24) | I will come and heal him <sup>NASB ESV NET</sup> · Shall I come and heal him? <sup>NIV</sup> |

**Greek lesson:** the oldest manuscripts have no question marks; editors add them. This line can be read two ways:

- **"I will come and heal him."** Jesus agrees at once.
- **"Shall *I* come and heal him?"** A Jewish teacher entering a Gentile's house would be unclean. The emphatic "I" sounds like surprise, and Jesus does the same with another Gentile in 15:21–28.

The centurion's answer fits either: "I'm not worthy for you to come under my roof." **Choice 4.**

## Patterns worth highlighting

**Repeated words** (Greek shown at each one):

- **under** ×3: roof, authority, me. The core of his argument.
- **boy** ×3: 8:6, 8:8, 8:13.
- **will be cured / was cured**: promise, then fulfillment.
- **trust / trusted**: a page-wide color.
- **Go!/goes · Come!/comes · Do!/does**: three pairs, each its own match.
- **thrown down / thrown out**: same root.
- *Maybe:* **say a word / I say to this one / Truly I say to you**, whose word has authority. Possibly too many highlights.

**Page-wide colors:** those following (purple) · Go (purple, like 8:4) · amazed · trust.

**One-off echoes:** authority (7:29) · not worthy (John, 3:11) · east (the Magi) · darkness (4:16) · let it be done (6:10).

## A structure to react to

Boxes and thin lines, no letters:

```
┌ 8:5–6   The plea: approaching, "Master, my boy has been thrown down…"
│ ─────
└ 8:7     Jesus: "I will come and heal him" (or "Shall I…?")
┏ 8:8–9   The centurion's word:
┃           not worthy / under my roof / just say a word
┃         ─────
┃           for I too am under authority, with soldiers under me:
┃              "Go!"  and he goes
┃              "Come!" and he comes
┗              "Do this!" and he does
┌ 8:10–12 Jesus' word: amazed / "such trust"
│         ─────
│           many from east and west → recline with Abra'am…
└           the sons of the kingdom → thrown out into the darkness
┏ 8:13    "Go. As you have trusted, let it be done for you."
┗           And the boy was cured in that hour.
```

The middle is two speeches facing each other: the centurion's word about authority, and Jesus' word about trust. 8:11 and 8:12 sit at the same indent, so "in with Abra'am" and "out into the darkness" read as a pair.

## Choices

1. **ἑκατόνταρχος:** "centurion" (familiar) or "hundred-commander" (Tim-style compound)?
2. **παρακαλέω:** "pleading with him"? It breaks Tim's "comfort" (5:4), but that's a different sense. The root is "calling alongside".
3. **παῖς:** "boy" (keeps the ambiguity between son and servant; Matthew uses "slave" separately in 8:9), "servant", or "son"? The row title says "son".
4. **8:7:** statement ("I will come and heal him") or question ("Shall *I* come and heal him?")?
5. **Two healing words:** θεραπεύω "heal" (Tim); ἰάομαι **"cure"** ("will be cured", "was cured"). "Made well" is saved for σῴζω (9:21–22).
6. **Two "go" verbs:** the centurion's πορεύομαι and Jesus' ὑπάγω. Tim uses "go" for both; keep "go" for both and show the Greek on each.
7. **βάλλω / ἐκβάλλω:** "thrown down" (8:6) and "thrown out" (8:12)? It carries forward: demons "thrown out" (8:16, 31; 9:33), and the Lord of the harvest "throws out workers" (9:38). Tim varies ("take out" the speck, "cast out" demons), so it's our call.

## Tenses: "approaching" and "approached"

Both are the **same tense** (aorist, a single completed action). What differs is the kind of verb form:

| Greek | Form | Job in the sentence | Ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| προσελθὼν (8:2) | aorist **participle** | describes the man on the way to the main action | approaching |
| προσῆλθεν (8:5) | aorist **finite verb** | the main action itself | approached |

English "-ing" here shows a participle, not the present tense; it's how Tim renders προσελθὼν (4:3) and how we did ἐκτείνας, "stretching out" (8:3, also aorist). We considered "having approached" and kept "-ing": the aorist sees an action as one whole event; it doesn't say "before". "Having" adds a time claim the Greek doesn't always make: ἀποκριθεὶς … ἔφη (8:8) is "responding, he said", not "having responded, he said", and the same Greek form needs the same English everywhere. When a participle opens a sentence with its own subject (the genitive at the start of 8:1 and 8:5), English uses "when…": "Now when he entered". Rule: **aorist participle → "-ing"; aorist finite verb → simple past; opening genitive participle → "when…"**.

## What we decided (8:5–6)

- **"Now when he entered into Kapharnaoum"**: δέ "Now", like 8:1. Εἰσελθόντος is aorist, so "entered", not "was entering": the same shape as "Now when he came down from the mountain".
- **"a centurion / approached him, / pleading with him and saying"**: here the main verb is προσῆλθεν "approached", and "pleading" and "saying" are participles, the reverse of 8:2 ("approaching, he bowed down"). The lines still parallel 8:2. "With" isn't italicized: English needs it to say "plead with" (same rule as "when"). "And saying" keeps the καί; verse 6 starts on it.
- **"my boy (παῖς son / servant)"**: like clean / pure, one word in the text and the second meanings in the bracket. Blue, three times (8:6, 8, 13).
- **"has been thrown down"**: βέβληται is a perfect, a past action whose result still stands. "Is thrown down" could sound like it's happening now. Light red, paired with ἐκβληθήσονται "thrown out" (8:12).
- **"in the house"**: the Greek has "the" (τῇ), not "my".
- **Plead is page-wide**: light violet on "pleading with" (8:5), and on παρεκάλουν (8:31, the demons) and παρεκάλεσαν (8:34, the town begs him to leave). Three pleas: for healing, for escape, for his absence.
- **Approach is page-wide**: light turquoise on "approaching" (8:2) and "approached" (8:5), and in the Greek of 8:19, 25; 9:14, 20, 28 (marked now).
- **Boxes, first pass, parallel to 8:1–4:** 8:5a bridge (light box) / the plea, Jesus' answer, and the centurion's word in one strong box with thin lines / Jesus to his followers, 8:10–12 (light box, an aside) / 8:13 the "Go" and the cure (strong box). The two strong boxes are the healing; the light box between them is the teaching.

## Two things for later

- **A feast button.** 8:11 is an echo: Isaiah 25:6, the feast for all nations on the mountain. It could get a dashed button.
- **Three outsiders in a row.** The leper, the Gentile, and next a woman (8:14–15). None of them could go into the inner courts of the Temple, and Jesus goes to all three first.
