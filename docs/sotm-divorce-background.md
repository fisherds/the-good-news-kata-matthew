# Background for Matthew 5:31–32 (case study 3, divorce)

From an earlier conversation with Claude (claude.ai/share/8c15445f-e315-4476-92ec-cbb224bc46d3). **Not yet checked against the sources**: verify each citation before it goes into an ⓘ pop-up (evidence, not opinion).

Why it matters: understand the world Jesus spoke into first, to see his wisdom, rather than applying the words straight to today.

## The audience

- The crowd included women and families (5:1, 7:28), but this case study speaks to men: under Deuteronomy 24:1–4 only the husband could issue a certificate of divorce.
- The live debate: Shammai (divorce only for serious sexual misconduct) vs Hillel (for "any cause", even a spoiled meal). Matthew 19:3 is the Pharisees asking Jesus exactly the "any cause" question.

## Marriage

- First marriages: overwhelmingly arranged by fathers / families (betrothal contract, bride-price). No statistics exist; "90%+" can't be proven but describes the norm. A groom often had some input; an older man marrying again likely chose.
- Ages: wives betrothed in their early-to-mid teens, married by mid-to-late teens. Husbands: rabbinic ideal 18 (Mishnah Avot 5:21); Michael Satlow argues many married mid-20s to about 30.
- Lifespan: low averages are mostly infant deaths; men who reached adulthood often lived into their 50s–60s.
- Picture: a man in his late 20s to 40s, a wife about 10 years younger, who had little say in the marriage and no power to end it. Divorce could leave her without support.

## Adultery

- No numbers. Likely uncommon in daily life: village life had no privacy, women's movements were watched, and the cost was catastrophic (disgrace, loss of her ketubah settlement, in theory death, Leviticus 20:10).
- Caution: Mishnah Sotah 9:9 says Yohanan ben Zakkai stopped the Numbers 5 bitter-water ordeal because adulterers had become so many. Maybe exaggerated, but the rabbis thought it a real problem.

## Divorce was easy and ordinary

- Josephus (90s AD) treats divorce "for any cause whatsoever" as normal and mentions divorcing his own wife because he was displeased with her behavior (Life 426).
- Sirach 25:26: cut off a wife who doesn't do as you wish.
- Mishnah Ketubot 7:6: a man may divorce without paying her settlement if she went out with her hair unbound or chatted with men in public. Suspicion, not proof.
- A whole tractate (Gittin) on divorce certificates; surviving documents from the Judean desert (Murabba'at 19, about AD 71).
- Against overstating it: the husband usually had to repay the ketubah, a real deterrent for poor men; more marriages ended by death (often childbirth) than divorce.
- No divorce rate is known. For teaching: "every man in that crowd knew someone who had done it, and some had done it themselves."

## What that does to Jesus' words

The wives were mostly faithful women, married young without much choice, who could be sent away for a burnt meal, a rumor, or a man's wandering eye, and then had to remarry or risk poverty. Jesus shuts nearly all the easy exits and leaves only πορνεία, stricter than Shammai in practice; hence the disciples' "it is better not to marry" (19:10). The weight falls on protecting wives: he makes her a victim of adultery.
