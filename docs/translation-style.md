# How Tim Mackie translates Matthew 1–7

What we learned by comparing BibleProject's 30 literary-design units for Matthew 1:1–7:29 with the SBLGNT Greek and with NIV (2011), ESV, NASB95 and NET. This is the style guide for continuing the translation past 7:29 (`/extendedreader`).

Working file: `data/compare/matthew-1-7.md` (gitignored — it holds the full English translations). Each unit shows Tim's text one line per USX group (**bold** = highlighted, `[v]` = verse marker), then SBLGNT and the four translations for the same range.

## 1. He translates from the Greek, not from an English Bible

About a quarter of Tim's 3-word phrases also appear in each translation (all 29 units: NIV 25%, ESV 28%, NASB95 27%, NET 26%). No translation is his base. Overlap is lowest in the narrative of chapters 1–4 (often 10–26%) and highest in well-known teaching where the Greek leaves little choice (5:43–48: ESV 64%, NASB95 67%; 7:24–27: ESV 64%). Occasionally a line is just ESV (6:16 "don't look gloomy like the hypocrites").

So for us: work from the Greek. Use the four translations to check meaning and to catch mistakes, not as wording to copy.

## 2. Principles

1. **One Greek word, one English word (concordance).** Repeated Greek roots stay visibly repeated in English, so the reader can see what the Greek reader sees. γεννάω "birth" everywhere (Abraam *birthed* Isaak; what has been *birthed* inside her; she will *birth* a son), γένεσις "birth-beginning" (1:18) and "birth-generations" (1:1). This is the main reason his English sounds unusual.
2. **Follow the Greek word order and grammar.** Participles stay participles ("having been engaged", "being a righteous one", "and responding, he said", "approaching, the tester said"). Hebrew-style idioms stay literal ("have in the womb", "he did not know her", "call his name"). Closer to NASB than to NIV, and more literal than NASB in places.
3. **Hyphenated compounds for one Greek word that needs several English words:** filled-full (πληρόω), birth-beginning, moral-failings (ἁμαρτίαι), doing-what-is-right (δικαιοσύνη, sometimes), change-direction (μετανοέω), stored-wealth (θησαυρός), body-parts (μέλη), court-judge (κριτής), threshing-fork, animal-skin, grain-husks, thorn-bushes, tormenting-pain, shone-in-appearance, death-end, life-span, inner-room, sent-away-woman, genuine-and-generous / bad-and-greedy (the healthy/bad eye, 6:22–23).
4. **Names are transliterated from the Greek**, not the familiar English forms. See section 4.
5. **Spoken, informal English.** Contractions (don't, won't, you're, doesn't), "y'all" for plural ὑμεῖς where English "you" would be ambiguous (3:7–12, 5:13, 6:25), "Look" for ἰδού (never "behold"), "Truly I say to you" for ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν.
6. **Reframes familiar religious words** so they sound fresh: "messenger" (ἄγγελος), "immerse" (βαπτίζω), "change direction" (μετανοέω), "the skies" (οὐρανοί), "the slanderer" (διάβολος), "Messiah" (never "Christ"), "Torah" (νόμος), "moral-failings" (ἁμαρτίαι), "How good is life for…" (μακάριοι).

## 4. Names

Greek forms, transliterated simply (h-less, with -os/-es/-a endings kept), with ’ marking a dropped Hebrew guttural:

Iesous, Maria, Ioseph, Ioannes (the immerser), Dauid, Abra’am, Isaak, Iakob, Iouda/Ioudaia, Ioudeans, Bethle’em, Hierosoluma (once Ierosoluma — inconsistent), Herodes, Arkhelaos, Rakhel, Rama, Nazaret / Nazara, Nazoraios, Galilaia, Iordanes, Kapharnaoum, Zaboulon, Nephthalim, Dekapolis, Esaias (Isaiah), Ieremias (Jeremiah), Petros, Andreas, Iakobos, Zebedaios, Pharisaions / Saddoukaions (Greek genitive plural, 3:7). Genealogy: Phares, Zara, Esrom, Aram, Aminadab, Nasson, Boes, Rakhab, Iobed, Iessai, Ouriah, Roboam, Abia, Iosaphat, Ioram, Ozia, Iotham, Akhaz, Ezekia, Manasse, Iosia, Iekhonia, Salathiel, Zorobabel, Abioud, Akhim, Elioud, Sadok.

Kept in familiar form: Israel, Emmanuel, Egypt, Babylon. Titles and terms are translated or kept as Hebrew-world words rather than transliterated from Greek: Messiah, Torah, Sanhedrin, gehenna.

## 5. Layout (USX)

- **One clause per line** (`<group>`). Median 7 words a line; 90% are 4–11 words.
- **Indent** (`indent="1"`–`"4"`) shows what depends on what: main clause, then the clause that hangs on it, and so on.
- **Boxes:** `type="primary"` (dark border) for main blocks, `type="secondary"` (light border) for related or parallel blocks, often alternating so parallels sit side by side visually.
- **Sidebar letters** (`sidebar-value`) mark parallels and chiasms: A B A′ B′, a b c d / a′ b′ c′ d′ / a″ b″ c″ d″ (6:1–4 is the cleanest example).
- **Group titles** label sub-scenes and quotations: "Test 1", "Deuteronomy 8:3", "Salt", "Social Status".
- `<optbreak />` draws a thin rule between sense-units inside a box.
- **Verse markers** sit at the start of the line where the verse begins. In the genealogy (1:1–17) the markers count the generations instead (1–14, three times), and 4:23–24 has a numbered 1–7 list — markers are not always verse numbers.

## 6. Highlighting

- Half of the highlights mark **a word or phrase repeated in the unit** (birth, son, Ioseph; "will be guilty" ×4 in 5:21–22; "it has been written" ×4 in 4:1–11). The other half mark **key one-off phrases** (the quotation formula, the place names, the climax).
- **Colors are chosen per unit**, not fixed per concept ("the skies" is cyan in some units, blue in others). Tendencies: cyan = the skies / kingdom / your Father; purple = son, Dauid, Iesous (messianic identity); orange = birth, reward; black = structural formulas ("You have heard that it was said", "And I say to you", "because").
- **Variants:** `highlight` (solid), `highlight-light` (tint, the most common), `highlight-dark` (strong, e.g. the repeated "don't" in 6:1–4), `text` (colored text only, for longer phrases).
- **`word-original`** shows the Greek on hover for a word whose repetition matters (θησαυρίζω, ἀντί, πληρόω, γένεσις).

## 7. Where Tim is inconsistent

Useful because it shows what's a rule and what's a slip:

- **6:1–4, 6:5–6 and 6:16–18 are three copies of one pattern** (when you do X, don't be like the hypocrites, they have their reward, but you in hiddenness, your Father will repay). 6:5–6 is laid out best; 6:1–4 has full a/b/c/d structure; 6:16–18 has no structure and different wording for the same Greek ("in a hidden way" for ἐν τῷ κρυφαίῳ, "reward" for ἀποδώσει). A fixed 6:16–18 for `/sotm` is on the to-do list (our own copy; Tim's file stays untouched).
- δικαιοσύνη: "righteousness" (5:6, 10) vs "doing-what-is-right / doing-right" (3:15, 5:20, 6:1, 6:33).
- ἀμὴν λέγω: "Truly I say to you" vs "Truly, I tell you".
- πληρόω: "fill-full" almost always, once "fulfilled".
- Hierosoluma / Ierosoluma.
