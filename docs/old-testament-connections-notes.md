# Connection to the Old Testament (Matthew 1:1–4:17)

Notes on how the Friday Bible study teaches this section (from the "Matthew 1:1–4:16 Connection to the OT" slides, Sept–Oct 2026) and the decisions behind `/otconnections`.

## Where it sits

The section **before** Matthew's five teaching blocks, ending where "Jesus began to announce: change direction, because the kingdom of the skies has come near!" (4:17). That line and 16:21 ("Jesus began to show…") are the book's two hinges. 4:17 belongs to both `/otconnections` and `/announcingkingdom`, as in Tim's units (4:12–17 and 4:17–5:2). The slides say 1:1–4:16; the page uses 4:17 to match Tim.

Five parts ("only five parts, easy"):

| | Part | Verses | Sub-parts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Genealogy | 1:1–17 | |
| 2 | Birth + Magi | 1:18–2:23 | Birth of Jesus 1:18–25 · Magi visit 2:1–12 · Herod 2:13–23 |
| 3 | John the Baptist | 3:1–17 | |
| 4 | Wilderness | 4:1–11 | Tests 1–3 |
| 5 | Capernaum | 4:12–17 | |

Birth (1:18–25) to Magi is under two years; Magi to John the Baptist jumps to Jesus at about thirty.

## The real story: the Old Testament quotes

The five parts are the structure, but the **seven prophet quotes** are the point, the way the videos are the special thing on `/sotm`. Matthew is "trying to winsomely persuade Jewish communities to accept Jesus as the true fulfillment of everything they've hoped for" (Tim Mackie).

7 prophet quotes + Jesus' 3 = 10 explicit OT references. Five of the seven are "filled-full" (πληρόω) quotes; Matthew has ten of those in all (1:22, 2:15, 2:17, 2:23, 4:14, then 8:17, 12:17, 13:35, 21:4, 27:9), plus Jesus' own 3:15 ("fill-full all doing-right") and 5:17 ("to fill them full", the Torah and the Prophets).

### Three stages of reading a quote

1. **Simple surface reading** — happy, easy, fine. ("Virgin + sign = done.")
2. **Scandalous realization** that your expectations are incomplete — go read the OT context and it doesn't say what you assumed (Isaiah 7's sign was a 13-year timeline for Ahaz; Hosea 11 looks back, it isn't a prediction).
3. **The patterns** Matthew is highlighting, how Jesus fills them full, and the nuanced complexity of Matthew and the Bible.

The image for it: we expect Matthew to be a lawyer or a professor proving A → B (Q.E.D.); he's really Rafiki or Yoda — surprising at first, and more than you thought later.

Keep perspective: "Bible nerd fun" (playful OT references we have to research) vs the weightier application coming later in Matthew. Be mindful of time spent.

### What the slides say about each quote

1. **Immanuel.** Isaiah 7–8: Ahaz, threatened by Rezin of Aram and Pekah of Israel (the Syro-Ephraimite war, 735–732 BC), had already paid off Tiglath-Pileser III of Assyria, and refuses to ask for a sign. The sign is a timeline: before the boy knows good from evil, the two kings are gone, and Assyria comes for Judah too. Matthew uses the time marker as the sign. The recurring problem is not trusting that God is with us; Jesus fills full "God with us". (The almah/betulah debate: "overdone already" — Genesis 24:16 vs 24:43.)
2. **Bethlehem.** Not "filled-full", and it's the chief priests and scribes who quote it. A quote fusion: Micah 5:2 (ruler from little Bethlehem) + 2 Samuel 5:2 ("you will shepherd my people Israel"). Jesus is the new King David.
3. **Egypt.** Hosea 11 is a lament looking back: called out → forgets → spirals back (Baals, Egypt, Assyria) → collapse. Jesus breaks the pattern: called out and stays out. Done by wordplay.
4. **Rachel.** Rama is where the exiles were gathered for Babylon. Mothers grieving under a cruel ruler keeps recurring, but Jeremiah 31 goes on to hope ("your children will return"); Jesus fills full that hope.
5. **Nazareth.** "The prophets" (plural) — no single verse. Ναζωραῖος echoes netzer (branch, Isaiah 11:1), nazir (set apart, Judges 13:5), and the despised small town (Psalm 22:6, Isaiah 53:3; "can anything good come from Nazareth?"). "The closest to an all-out bit of humor."
6. **John the Baptist.** Isaiah 40:3.
7. **Zebulun → Naphtali.** Jesus moves from Nazareth (Zebulun) to Capernaum (Naphtali). "Preaches fine by itself": the light rising in the land first lost to Assyria, and Isaiah 9 goes on to the child born to rule.

### "Fives within fives"

Five parts; five prophet references in chapters 1–2; five dreams in 1–2 (1:20, 2:12, 2:13, 2:19, 2:22; then 27:19); Bethlehem five times in chapter 2 (2:1, 5, 6, 8, 16). Matthew is built like the five books of Moses (the five teaching blocks), and Jesus is the new Moses.

## The genealogy

- 14 / 14 / 14: Abraham → David → the exile → the Messiah. David in Hebrew (דוד) adds up to 14. 41 names (one repeated) lead to #42.
- Four surprises (in red in Tim's design): Tamar, Rakhab, Ruth, "the one of Ouriah" — plus Maria. Rescued-by-outsiders and adoption genealogy?
- Surprising kings: Solomon (1 Kings 9:4–8, the "if"), Jeconiah (Jeremiah 22:24–30, "write this man down as childless"). Luke's line goes through Nathan and Neri instead, avoiding both.
- Names that look swapped: Asa → Asaph (the poet), Amon → Amos (the prophet). Omitted kings: Ahaziah, Joash, Amaziah, Jehoiakim.
- Shealtiel has two fathers (Jeconiah in Matthew, Neri in Luke): don't force biology on genealogies (levirate marriage, Obed; "my son" Timothy, Mark; Augustus).
- Possible goals: paternal, kingly, nationalism-lampooning ("do not say 'we have Abraham as our father'", 3:9), rescued by non-Israelite women, adoption.
- Early tradition (Papias, Irenaeus) says Matthew first wrote in Hebrew.

## Page decisions (`/otconnections`)

- Same pattern as `/sotm`: outline table, 📖 panels with Tim's text, Greek word counts (Textus Receptus), verse counts, bars, collapsible sections, `n` display modes.
- The OT quotes take the place of the videos column. Each is a numbered circle (1–7, J1–J3): **filled** = filled-full formula, **outlined** = names the prophet without filled-full (2, 6), **dark** = Jesus' "it has been written". Clicking opens a pop-up with Tim's text of the Matthew verses, the Greek formula, the OT passages (BSB, public domain, focus verses highlighted) and a one-line note from the slides.
- Quote data: `public/data/otconnections_quotes.json`, built by `tools/fetch_ot_quotes.py` (edit the quotes there and re-run).
- Ideas not done yet: sub-rows for Herod (2:13–15 Egypt, 2:16–18 Rachel, 2:19–23 Nazareth) so each quote sits on its own row; sub-rows for the genealogy's three fourteens (1:6 and 1:11 split mid-verse, so they'd need half-verse ranges); the "fives" (dreams, Bethlehem) as a second kind of marker.
