# The nine miracle stories: a second structure, 4 + 4

The main structure of Matthew 8–9 is **3-3-3**: three groups of three stories, divided by the discipleship rows (Follow me 8:18–22; A meal with Matthew 9:9–13; Fasting 9:14–17). That stays the primary outline on `/ninemiraclestories`.

Underneath it is a second one, found while writing the 8:14–17 notes (2026-11-07) and shaped by the user: **stories 1–4 and stories 5–8 mirror each other pair by pair**, with the last two stories (the blind men and the mute man) counting together as one, the 8th.

| | First half | Second half |
|---|---|---|
| **1 ↔ 5** | 1. The leper (8:1–4) | 5. The two demonized men at Gadara (8:28–34) |
| **2 ↔ 6** | 2. The centurion's boy (8:5–13) | 6. The paralyzed man on the bed (9:1–8) |
| **3 ↔ 7** | 3. Peter's mother-in-law (8:14–15) | 7. The ruler's daughter and the bleeding woman (9:18–26) |
| **4 ↔ 8** | 4. The storm: chaos calmed (8:23–27) | 8. The two blind men (9:27–31) + the mute man (9:32–34) |

The two halves are bricks, not walls, like the groups on the Matthew page: both structures are true at once.

All Greek below is checked against SBLGNT (`data/matthew_greek.txt`).

## 1 ↔ 5: unclean outsiders (the softest pair: more theme than shared words)

- **Both are unclean and cut off from their people.** The leper lives "outside the camp" (Leviticus 13:45–46). The two men live in the tombs (ἐκ τῶν μνημείων, 8:28), which means corpse uncleanness (Numbers 19:16), near pigs (unclean, Leviticus 11:7), in Gentile land.
- **Both requests start with "if".** ἐὰν θέλῃς "if you want" (8:2) / Εἰ ἐκβάλλεις ἡμᾶς "if you throw us out" (8:31).
- **Jesus answers with a short command, including "Go".** Θέλω, καθαρίσθητι … ὕπαγε (8:3–4) / Ὑπάγετε (8:32), one word.
- **Both end with the community.** The leper is sent "as a witness to them" (εἰς μαρτύριον αὐτοῖς, 8:4). The herders "report everything" in the town (ἀπήγγειλαν πάντα, 8:33), and "the whole town" comes out to meet Jesus, and pleads with him to leave (8:34).
- A contrast inside the pair: the leper bows down (προσεκύνει, 8:2); the demons shout "What do we have to do with you, son of God?" (8:29).

## 4 ↔ 8: the strongest pair (the storm, then the blind men + the mute man)

Story 4 opens like story 8 and ends like story 9:

| | 4. Storm (8:23–27) | 8. Two blind men (9:27–31) | 9. Mute man (9:32–34) |
|---|---|---|---|
| opening | Καὶ **ἐμβάντι αὐτῷ** εἰς πλοῖον **ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ** "to him getting into a boat, they followed him" | Καὶ **παράγοντι** ἐκεῖθεν **τῷ Ἰησοῦ ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ** "to Iesous passing on from there, they followed him" | |
| approach | προσελθόντες (8:25) | προσῆλθον αὐτῷ (9:28) | |
| the cry | Κύριε, σῶσον "Master, save!" | Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς "Have mercy on us!" … Ναί, κύριε "Yes, Master" | |
| trust, then "then" | ὀλιγόπιστοι; **τότε** ἐγερθεὶς… "little-trusting ones? Then, getting up…" | Πιστεύετε…; **τότε** ἥψατο… "Do you trust…? Then he touched…" | |
| the ending | οἱ δὲ ἄνθρωποι **ἐθαύμασαν λέγοντες**· "What sort is this…?" | | καὶ **ἐθαύμασαν** οἱ ὄχλοι **λέγοντες**· "Never has it appeared like this in Israel" |

- **The two openings share an unusual shape.** Both put Jesus in the dative with a participle and follow it with "they followed him". No other story in the nine opens like this. (8:1 also has ἠκολούθησαν αὐτῷ, after a genitive absolute.)
- **The only two crowd endings with ἐθαύμασαν λέγοντες**, "they were amazed, saying": 8:27 and 9:33. In 8:10 Jesus is amazed, alone.
- **Both endings ask who he is**: "What sort is this?" / "Never like this in Israel". In 9:34 the Pharisees give the dark answer to the same question: "by the ruler of the demons".
- So 4 is the middle of the first half, and 8 + 9 together mirror it at the end of the second.

## Across the halves: "Go" only in the first two of each half

ὑπάγω ("go") appears in Matthew 8–9 only in **8:4 (story 1), 8:13 (story 2), 8:32 (story 5), 9:6 (story 6)**. It fits the 4 + 4 layout exactly; 3-3-3 doesn't explain it. Each half ends on a "who is this?" amazement (8:27; 9:33).

### Card titles (the user's)

In the Genesis style ("Day 1: Environment = Skies/Heavens"): **number of people, who, = the pair's theme.**

| Left | Right | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Story 1: 1 Leper | Story 5: 2 Demonized men | = Healing an Outsider |
| Story 2: 1 Centurion's boy | Story 6: 2 The paralyzed man and his friends | = Healing the thrown down |
| Story 3: 1 Mother-in-law | Story 7: 2 Daughters | = Healing life-giving generations |
| Story 4: 0 Chaos waters | Stories 8, 9: 3 Infirmed | = Giving life out of non-life |

**The counts climb: 1 → 2, 1 → 2, 1 → 2, then 0 → 3.** The first four stories are about one person each (or none, the storm); the second four are about two (and the last card three). Story 6 counts two on purpose, the paralyzed man **and** his friends: the user wants it to be about two groups, to keep the 1 → 2 pattern.

The Greek supports the counts:

- **Story 6, two groups**: "seeing **their** trust" (τὴν πίστιν **αὐτῶν**, 9:2), the friends' trust, not only his.
- **Story 7, two daughters**: the ruler says "my **daughter**" (Ἡ θυγάτηρ μου, 9:18), and Jesus calls the bleeding woman "**Daughter**" (Θάρσει, θύγατερ, 9:22). Two daughters in the Greek.
- **Stories 8 + 9, three**: two blind men (δύο τυφλοὶ, 9:27) and one mute man (9:32).
- **Story 4, zero**: nobody is healed. Jesus calms the sea itself.

More links these titles bring out:

- **2 ↔ 6, the "child" words.** The centurion's παῖς "boy" (8:6) and the paralyzed man's "Take courage, **child**" (Θάρσει, τέκνον, 9:2).
- **6 ↔ 7, "Take courage."** Θάρσει opens Jesus' words to the paralyzed man (9:2) and to the woman (9:22), and nowhere else in the nine.
- **3 ↔ 7, generations.** 10:35 uses both words of this pair: "a **daughter** against her mother, and a daughter-in-law against her **mother-in-law**" (θυγατέρα … πενθερᾶς). These are the only two uses of πενθερά in Matthew (8:14; 10:35).

Spelling for the build: Centurion, Chaos, "Giving life out of non-life". Story 5 could say "Demonized" (Tim's word, and Choice 6 in the 8:14–17 notes) instead of "Demon possessed" to match the text. That's the user's call: row titles may use familiar names.
